Case Study 04 / 2024

The Silent Structure

Location Reykjavík, Iceland
Scale 4,200 SQM / Cultural Center
Phase Completed Dec 2023

A dialogue between mass and nothingness.

Designed as a sanctuary for silence, The Silent Structure emerges from the volcanic landscape of Iceland not as an intrusion, but as an extension of the earth’s own tectonic narrative. The architecture explores the concept of 'The Void'—treating empty space as a tangible material.

By utilizing heavy, light-absorbing surfaces, we've created a series of chambers that manipulate acoustics and natural light to evoke a sense of permanence and introspection. The building does not shout; it hums with the frequency of the surrounding tundra.

light_mode

Chiaroscuro in Concrete

The passage of time is measured by the movement of light across the raw concrete surfaces. Each aperture is calculated to capture specific celestial alignments during the winter solstice.

The Material Palette

The texture of permanence.

01. Raw Concrete

Cast-on-site using local volcanic aggregates, the concrete retains the fingerprint of the environment.

02. Charred Wood

Traditional Japanese Yakisugi method used on Cedar panels to provide natural fire resistance and deep tonal contrast.

03. Cold-Pressed Steel

Untreated steel fixtures that will oxidize over decades, documenting the building's age through color shift.

Environmental Ethics

Architecture as a living ecosystem.

The structure utilizes subterranean thermal mass to maintain constant temperatures without active cooling or heating. Rainwater is harvested through the central void, filtered through volcanic stone, and recirculated within the building's gray water systems.
